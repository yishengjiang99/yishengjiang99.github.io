Full-stack agentic AI engineer, putting tool calls wherever they run best.

I build fast, AI-powered camera, audio, and video applications across servers, browsers, and edge devices—including edge GPUs.

An agent is only as good as where its tools run. I place each part of the agentic loop—the model call, the tool execution, and the inference behind it—on the compute tier that serves it best. Heavy FFmpeg jobs and frontier models belong on the server. Microphone-adjacent processing belongs in the browser. Latency-sensitive, private, or high-volume inference belongs on the iPhone.

The default is local first, with the cloud as a deliberate fallback rather than a reflex.

On the server, FinalCut gives Grok more than forty function-calling tools over FFmpeg. Ask it to “subtitle the song in Chinese,” and the lyric-captions tool uploads only the audio track, transcribes it with word-level timestamps through Whisper, asks Grok to identify the song, corrects transcription errors against published lyrics, and translates each line. The result is a set of synchronized, two-line bilingual captions ready to burn into the video.

On the iPhone, the tools run natively. FinalCap, FinalCut’s iOS sibling, executes twenty-one of the same tool calls on-device using AVFoundation and Core Image, while Apple’s on-device speech recognition generates captions. If a tool has no native implementation, it can fall back to the server—but only when Cloud Processing is explicitly enabled. It is off by default.

ProTune AI Camera follows the same architecture. A Vision pass analyzes the live viewfinder, and voice notes are transcribed locally. Grok enters the loop only for higher-level refinement: it must enumerate the available photography recipes, inspect relevant candidates, and select one. Its output is translated directly into shutter speed, ISO, white balance, focus, and zoom targets on the active camera session.

Music Reader takes the idea further: it reads a photograph of sheet music entirely offline. Its recognition pipeline splits inference between Core ML and ONNX Runtime, while the decoder keeps its KV cache resident across token steps so intermediate state never has to round-trip through Swift.

In the browser, AI Music Radar runs its transcription model on WebGPU, with WebGL and WASM fallbacks, while microphone capture happens inside an AudioWorklet. On iPhone, the same Basic Pitch model runs through Core ML.

Real-time performance is the common constraint across all of these systems. The SoundFont synthesizer shared by the music applications renders audio without allocating memory or taking locks on the realtime thread, while earlier browser experiments pair AudioWorklets with WebAssembly for low-latency DSP.

The projects below break down these systems individually. Status notes are current as of late 2026.

ProTune AI Camera

App Store live · 1.1+ · 1.2 in review

ProTune (shipping name ProTune AI Camera; also listed historically as AI Camera — Auto Recipes) is a viewfinder-first iPhone camera. The home screen is the live preview, not a gallery of filters. You point at a scene, tap Auto Optimize, and the app writes real capture dials — shutter, ISO, EV, white balance, focus, and zoom — instead of painting beauty looks onto a JPEG after the fact.

The product problem is familiar to anyone who has collected “photo recipes” from blogs and still ended up guessing dial positions on a phone. ProTune bundles a small catalog of field recipes, then uses a fast on-device Vision pass plus an optional cloud refine — a Grok tool loop grounded in that catalog, so it cannot invent a recipe — to pick and apply the right one. Teach Mode explains which recipe won and why the dials moved. Pro unlocks unlimited Auto Optimize, manual A/S/M dials, and full teach sheets through StoreKit subscriptions; Free Peek keeps the viewfinder and a daily optimize quota.

Under the hood the interesting bits are the AVFoundation session that actually accepts those phone targets, on-device speech for voice notes into the same recommend loop, IAP entitlement plumbing, and Meta + SKAdNetwork attribution for the ads path. Bundle id com.ragnus.mvp. Companion marketing site at photo.grepawk.com.

AI Camera — Music Reader

TestFlight / App Store path · not publicly listed yet

Music Reader (home-screen name SheetCam) answers a different camera question: point at engraved Western sheet music and hear the page. The app runs optical music recognition entirely on device — SegNet and encoder models via Core ML, decoder on ONNX Runtime CPU — then emits SMF MIDI plus a note layout so the player can highlight what it is sounding.

Playback uses an SF2 SoundFont engine with tempo control, A–B loop, hand mute / solo, and a small instrument palette. Every successful scan lands in an on-device library. Free allows a handful of scans per day; Pro unlocks unlimited via subscription. No account, and photos never leave the phone for recognition.

The hard engineering is the locked pipeline: optional Vision crop, staff geometry and dewarp, zero-copy ORT I/O binding for the decoder KV-cache, and keeping memory and warmup budgets honest on real devices. Bundle id com.ragnus.vp. The App Store product page is not public yet, so the links below are the source repo and related privacy page only.

FinalCut / FinalCap

Web live · iOS port in progress

FinalCut is a chat-driven video editor hosted at grepawk.com. You upload clips, talk to an assistant about what you want, and the server runs FFmpeg for the actual transforms — trim, crop, resize, overlays, transitions between clips, color and a long list of audio filters. The point is not another timeline UI; it is “describe the edit, get a render.”

The same product thread continues as FinalCap on iOS (bundle family com.ragnus.w2), porting the editing surface closer to the phone. Auth and billing on the web side use Google OAuth and Stripe; the interesting product constraint is keeping FFmpeg jobs reliable while the chat layer stays useful instead of decorative.

AI Music Radar

Public repo · web demo live · iOS on TestFlight path

EarSheet / AI Music Radar is the sibling of Music Reader. Where SheetCam reads a printed page, Music Radar listens: microphone or imported audio goes through a fine-tuned Spotify Basic Pitch model in Core ML and comes out as an engraved staff, SMF MIDI, and MusicXML. Playback reuses the same SF2 engine. Audio stays on the phone.

There is also a browser demo that runs Basic Pitch on WebGPU / WebGL / WASM at grepawk.com/music-hear/. The iOS app identity is locked (bundle com.ragnus.pnge); it is public enough to talk about, still early relative to ProTune on the store.

Shared libraries

sf2player-swift is the shared SoundFont 2 rendering engine (Swift port of the gbk renderer) consumed by Music Reader and Music Radar. Keeping the synth as its own AGPL package means both apps pin a revision and stay out of each other’s player UI.

Earlier browser experiments

Before the iOS products, a lot of GrepAwk work lived as WASM demos: a browser SoundFont player, SF2 Explorer, wavetable oscillators, an algorithmic two-part invention, FFT spectrum and zero-copy audio, plus motion camera and BrowserCraft. They are still up if you want the archaeology.

Seal of the High Priestess

Playable in the browser · vertical slice

Seal of the High Priestess — A Journey of Purification and Poisoned Words — is a 2D JRPG / visual-novel hybrid that runs from a static page. Its design takes its cue from LinaHua’s solo, AI-assisted epic, in the spirit of Final Fantasy VI crossed with Fate/Stay Night: dialogue first, named gear, and slow, strategic combat. The party is Elara, a priestess; Kael, a sealed demon prince; Lyra, a scout; and Thorn, a bound bruiser. The current slice runs the full intro — temple, village, forest, Heartwood Hollow, the canal city of Meridia — through several thousand words of original visual-novel scenes, and stops at the outer gates of the Throne of Ash.

Combat is about tempo, not grinding. Elara spends Mana to Shield, Heal, or Empower; Meditating restores it but costs her turn and leaves her vulnerable. At full Mana she can Break the High Seal: Kael goes berserk for four turns, then Elara is gassed and cannot act for two, and many skills need charge-up turns first or leave you gassed afterward. There is no XP grind and no generic shop: strength comes from named gear and techniques, and Korin the smith reforges what you already carry rather than selling you something better. Story bosses include the Hollow Oak, the Canal Specter, the Gate Warden, the Bound Hound and The Unbetrayed; between them are optional quests, lotus-altar saves, a day/night tint, and a mid-game choice over whether to fully unseal Kael.

Technically it is deliberately plain: no engine, no CDN, no build step — a canvas and a small finite-state machine (title, visual novel, map, battle, menu) in vanilla JavaScript. Spoken dialogue uses pre-generated ElevenLabs clips, one voice per character, bundled as static MP3s (369 clips across 57 bundles today), so the browser never calls ElevenLabs. A script names each clip by a SHA-256 hash of its line, voice and settings, so only new or changed lines are regenerated, and a GitHub Actions workflow commits fresh clips back and rebuilds Pages. A Playwright headless suite runs in CI.
